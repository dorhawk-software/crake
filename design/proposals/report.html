A report to send when something's wrong

Proposal, 2026-10-18. When someone writes "I connected and my internet stopped", the person helping them needs to see that computer: how its DNS and routes are set up right then, what Crake added, whether the internet answers, and what craked logged. Today Copy for helpdesk gives Crake's decisions and a line per tunnel, nothing of the computer, and Export saves the log rows shown, without a word once it's done. The report puts it all in one file someone can send, from the machine where it happened.

When something's wrong
A file with what someone helping you needs to see: this computer's DNS and routes, what Crake added, whether the internet answers, and the logs.

What's in the file

Plain text anyone can read, always the same parts, whatever the log window shows (its search and filters don't cut it down): the last 24 hours of Crake's decisions, and craked's own log for the last hour or 200 lines. Crake sends it nowhere: whoever saved it decides who gets it.

  • Never in it, in any mode: sign-ins (session cookies, OpenVPN's tokens, sign-in page addresses), usernames and passwords, keyring items, a profile's text, its keys and certificates, and their paths.
  • With hiding on (Settings → Privacy & log), names, domains and the addresses of the hosts reached, IPv4 and IPv6, become "a host" in every part, as Copy for helpdesk does; the tunnel names stay, and say whose they are. The computer's own plumbing stays: its own addresses, each tunnel's address and DNS server, the network's router, Crake's 127.53.0.1. Without them the routes and DNS parts say nothing. The setting gets a title to match: "Hide names and addresses when sharing", "Copy for helpdesk and reports leave out the hosts you reached."
  • Other VPN clients: the DNS part lists every resolver on the machine, another VPN client's (and its company's domains) too, as the system has them. Hidden, their domains go as well.

The same lines, hidden:

ACME: connected for 12 min on utun4, address 10.40.255.50, DNS 10.40.0.2 for a host; 3 hosts learned, 1 pinned ACME's names: a host is a host; a connection on 443 took 31 ms, through ACME (utun4). resolver #8 a host → 127.53.0.1 Crake, for ACME a host utun4 Crake: learned 11 min ago

craked gathers the computer's part (it can read all of it); the app adds the rest. On Linux the DNS part is systemd-resolved's (resolvectl status) and the rules Crake's nftables table; on Windows the NRPT rules, the adapters' DNS and Crake's firewall rules.

When craked can't be asked (it isn't running, macOS still needs the service approved, or the user isn't the one at the screen), the file says so in that part ("craked isn't running: this computer's DNS, routes and Crake's rules weren't read.") and holds the rest: it always saves a file.

From a terminal too

crake report [file] writes the same file (by default crake-report-<date>.txt in Documents) and prints where; --hide-hosts or --show-hosts overrides the setting. No window opens, no sudo is needed, and without craked it writes what it can, as the app does. For helping someone over chat ("type crake report and send me the file"), or when the app won't open.

To decide

  • Placement: A, B or C.
  • The button's words: "Save a report…", or "Save for helpdesk…", which pairs with "Copy for helpdesk" and says who it's for.
  • Hidden, what stays: the computer's own plumbing (its addresses, the tunnels' addresses and DNS servers, the router), as proposed, or every address hidden, as Copy for helpdesk hides them today.
  • The panel too? Someone whose internet stopped opens the panel first; its footer could say "Save a report…" beside "Copy for helpdesk", or a tunnel in trouble could offer it. Today's proposal: the log only.

Recommendation: A, "Save a report…", the log only

People in trouble open the log, and Export already makes a file from it; a file with the computer's side is what the person helping them asks for next. One button changes its name and grows; nothing new to choose between. "Report" says what the file is, whoever it goes to (IT, a colleague, Crake's author), where "for helpdesk" names only one of them.
