Proposal: IPsec tunnels in New tunnel (CLAUDE.md, 7k)

Crake's service can now run IPsec (IKEv2) tunnels with strongSwan, on Linux. That's how FortiGates from FortiOS 7.6.3 on take remote workers, and many other gateways too. What's missing is a way to add one. For it, your IT team gives the gateway, the gateway's key (often called the pre-shared key) and your username; sometimes also the company's domains and the gateway's identity. “Today” is captured from the app at 1:1; the rest is drawn at the app's size. marks what changes. Two choices for you:
  1. Step 1 for every protocol: the protocol list folds once you've chosen one, and “Import .ovpn…” shows only while the protocol is “Detect automatically” or OpenVPN (1). This narrows what we decided on 2026-10-18 (“in every state”). Recommended.
  2. Where the company's domains go: step 1, under the key (A, recommended), or step 3 (B) (2).

1 · Step 1: IPsec chosen

A new card, “IPsec (IKEv2)”, under Beta until it's been used against a real gateway (your FortiGate first). Once a protocol is chosen, the list folds to that row and today's “Choose the protocol yourself” (today it folds only after detection answered or a .ovpn was imported). So the key, which IPsec needs before Continue, sits right under its gateway instead of 600 px further down, under the fold. Group's place, which IPsec doesn't use, takes the key and the domains, on the Name and Gateway grid.

2 · Your choice: where the company's domains go

AnyConnect and OpenVPN gateways hand Crake the company's domains; an IPsec gateway can't (strongSwan receives them and passes them on to no one). So for IPsec they're typed, when IT gives them.
AStep 1, under the key (as drawn in 1), recommended

3 · Step 2: how it signs in

IPsec signs in with a username and password (the gateway asks for them over EAP, as it asks FortiClient). So step 2 shows that one way as a plain card, as OpenVPN's step 2 shows “As the profile says”, with today's Username field. No “Stay signed in”: an IPsec gateway gives no session to keep, so the password is kept in your keyring instead: the sign-in window's “Remember the password in your keyring” is ticked from the start (5). The gateway's identity, which few gateways ask for, goes under today's Advanced.
