Traffic, live. A window of its own ("Traffic", from the panel's footer next to Full log). The graph is the point: this computer on the left, the ways out in the middle (direct, and each tunnel that's up), the busiest destinations on the right, with packets ticking along each path as they go. A path's dots come as often as its traffic flows; a host Crake has just sent through a tunnel gets its path drawn in. Under it, the last five minutes, and the tables as the detail.   • The graph. Paths from this computer to each way out, and on to the five busiest destinations of each (the rest as "and 18 more"). Dots move slowly, as fast as the path's traffic flows (a trickle crawls, a big download moves about twice as fast), and come one per ~250 KB: between one every four seconds and two a second, so the window can stay open beside work without pulling the eye. Direct is grey, tunnels teal, as in the panel. Pointing at a path or a host names it and its rate; clicking one finds it in the table below.
  • When Crake decides. A host Crake sends through a tunnel right now gets its path drawn from the tunnel to it, once (the panel's "What Crake did", seen happening). Undo moves it to direct the same way.
  • Last 5 minutes. The panel's chart, shorter and finer: a bar every 5 s, direct and through tunnels stacked, sliding left as time passes.
  • The tables. "Right now": open connections (the connection table craked already reads for probing), the app where the system says, and the rate. "Where things go": every rule (learned, pinned, never, sent direct with Undo, and everything else), the "tree" of the split, searchable.
  • Quiet. Only while the window is open (craked sends rates once a second then, nothing otherwise); with GNOME's animations off the dots stand still and the paths' thickness shows the rate instead. It stays on this computer like everything Crake sees; Copy for helpdesk never includes it.
