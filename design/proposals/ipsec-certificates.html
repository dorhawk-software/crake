Proposal: IPsec with certificates (CLAUDE.md, 7k)

Today an IPsec tunnel works one way only: the gateway proves itself with a key from IT (the pre-shared key), and you sign in with a username and password. Many gateways work differently. The gateway proves itself with a certificate, as a website does (Windows' built-in VPN works that way, and so do many FortiGates and strongSwan servers). Or you sign in with your own certificate, from a smart card, your keyring or a file, sometimes followed by your password. This proposal covers all three: the gateway's certificate, your certificate, and your certificate with a password. Untrusted gateway certificates are handled as you chose: when this computer doesn't know who signed the certificate, Crake asks, with AnyConnect's question. “Today” is drawn from the code (new_tunnel/ipsec.rs, widgets.rs, view.rs), at the app's size. marks what changes. Two choices for you:
  1. Step 1, how the gateway proves itself: two cards side by side, “A key” or “Its certificate” (A, recommended), or the key field as today with a link under it (B) (1).
  2. Step 2, your certificate and a password: a third card, “Certificate and password” (A, recommended), or a tick under “Certificate” (B) (2).
To prove before building: strongSwan doesn't hand over the certificate of a gateway it doesn't trust. Crake needs it for the trust question, and also to tell a wrong name from an unknown authority (5).

5 · What has to be proven first, and how it's built

To prove first (a short spike, before any UI)

  • The gateway's certificate. strongSwan checks the certificate against the authorities it has and logs only its name (“received end entity cert "CN=gw.nordbank.example"”), never the certificate itself. Its most detailed decryption log level dumps the gateway's decrypted replies, which carry it. So craked would read the certificate out of that dump. The trust question, the wrong-name failure (telling a wrong name from an unknown authority needs the names in the certificate) and the “changed” variant all depend on this. On “Trust it”, craked loads that one certificate as trusted for this connection alone. To prove: that the dump is whole (IKE fragments and long certificates included), and that a pinned certificate is trusted without its authority.
  • The dump holds secrets too. At that level strongSwan also dumps your own sign-in: the EAP identity and, with a password, a response that can be cracked. So the dump goes to craked's memory only, through VICI's log stream, and only for the gateway's first reply. It never reaches the journal, Crake's log, reports or Copy for helpdesk.
  • If the spike fails: there's no question, and no telling a wrong name from an unknown authority. Both end in today's failure shape: “Crake can't vouch for Nordbank's gateway. Ask your IT team for its certificate authority, or whether its name is right.”, with Choose IT's certificate authority…, which opens Edit at step 1. The rest of this proposal stands as drawn.
  • Your certificate, in IKE or over EAP. Some gateways take your certificate in IKE itself, others over EAP-TLS, and nothing tells which before you try. craked tries IKE first, then EAP-TLS when the gateway refuses it, and remembers what worked. To prove against strongSwan and a FortiGate.

How it maps onto strongSwan

  • The gateway's certificate: remote.auth = pubkey, with remote.id set to the gateway as typed (or the identity under Advanced). strongSwan's default, %any, would accept any trusted certificate for any name. The authorities are the system's (the distribution's CA bundle), or IT's file alone when one is given.
  • Your certificate: local.auth = pubkey. The app reads the certificate and its key from the keyring (or the file) and hands them to craked when it connects, as AnyConnect's are handed over today (craked runs as root and can't open your keyring). craked loads them over VICI, in memory only. A smart card goes through strongSwan's pkcs11 plugin, with the PIN as the token's secret. With a password as well, a second round: local-2.auth = eap (RFC 4739).
  • IT's files (managed.d): gateway_ca_file beside gateway_key_file, and sign_in = "certificate" or "certificate and password" with certificate, as AnyConnect's files have it. A file that names neither a key nor an authority trusts the system's authorities.
  • The sign-in window's steps become a list the tunnel gives (today a fixed three rows, signin_view/widgets.rs).
  • Privacy: Copy for helpdesk and reports carry the gateway certificate's names and fingerprint. Your own certificate's subject is often your full name and email address, so the copy without host names replaces it too. Neither ever carries a private key, a PIN or a password.
  • Tests: the e2e gateway gets an authority of its own and three connections: its certificate with a password, your certificate, and both. The scenario suite runs each, plus the trust question, a renewed certificate, a wrong name, an address, and certificates that have run out or aren't valid yet.
