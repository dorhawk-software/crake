# Consent without pressure

How an app asks for permission to collect or share something. What may
then leave the device, and when, is in
[crash-reports.md](../common/crash-reports.md).

## Two equal answers, no guilt

**What.**
- Consent is never pre-selected.
- A gentle visual preference is allowed. Both answers are the same size,
  equally readable, and free of guilt.
- Sensitive data goes past a lawyer before the screen ships.

**Why.** Scops is a baby-sleep app for tired parents, an audience that is
easy to pressure. On its anonymous-data screen (2026-11-14), the owner
wanted "yes" gently preferred. The line between a preference and a push
had to be written down, or the next round would cross it.

**Apply.**
1. Draw two equal buttons. The preferred one may carry the primary
   colour.
2. "No" carries no guilt and no cost: "No, thanks", never "I don't want
   to help".
3. Next to the buttons, say what is sent and what is never sent.
4. Changing your mind is one tap, in settings. The screen says where.
5. Nothing is collected before the answer. "No" means the collector
   never starts ([crash-reports.md](../common/crash-reports.md#consent-first-nothing-starts-before-the-user-agrees)).

**Check.** Both buttons have the same size and contrast. Neither is
selected when the screen opens. The screen says what is sent and where
to change the choice later.
